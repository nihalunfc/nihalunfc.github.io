Week 6 · Deliver BI people trust › Day 41

Day 41. Reconciliation, the skill that earns trust

⏱ 4h · learn 70m · lab 135m · ship 20m · review 15m🏷 retail, hospitality, marketingBefore: Day 40

By the end of today you can

  • Rebuild a reported number from raw data, step by step
  • Bridge two different totals with a variance waterfall
  • Run a "the numbers don't match" investigation with evidence
  • Set up certification and sign-off for important reports

1. Why this matters

Sooner or later someone says "your number is wrong". A junior defends the dashboard. A senior says "let's bridge it", and twenty minutes later shows exactly which differences are definitions, timing, scope and genuine errors. That skill is what makes managers trust a BI person, and it's a great interview story.

2. Why two totals differ

Almost every difference is one of these, in roughly this order of frequency:

  1. Definition: gross vs net, with or without tax, tips, delivery fees, refunds, comps.
  2. Timing: business date vs posting date vs calendar date; cut-off times; late files; provisional data.
  3. Scope: which stores/hotels/campaigns; closed or new locations; test transactions; currency.
  4. Data errors: duplicates, missing files, bad joins (fan-out), quarantined rows.
  5. Rounding: per line vs per receipt vs per day.

3. The bridge

Start from number A, apply each explained difference as a step, arrive at number B. What's left unexplained is the real problem.

Step Amount Evidence
Store system: September net sales, store R104 $412,880 Manager's report
− Sales tax included in store figure −$47,500 Store report is tax-inclusive (contract note)
− 30 Sept 23:00–01:00 sales booked to 1 Oct by business-day rule −$1,240 fct query by order_ts
+ Refunds netted by store, gross in BI +$2,150 refund rows in quarantine/refund table
− Duplicate email on 14 Sept removed by BI −$0 duplicate correctly ignored (manifest)
Explained BI figure $366,290
BI dashboard $366,140
Unexplained $150 investigate: 2 receipts in quarantine (bad timestamps)

Visualise it as a waterfall chart; finance people read waterfalls natively.

Maths and principle

Principle: a bridge decomposes a difference into additive, independently evidenced components; the residual is the test.

Equation: \(B - A = \sum_k \Delta_k + \varepsilon\). Each \(\Delta_k\) must be computed from data, not inferred from the gap. You're done when \(|\varepsilon|\) is below materiality (say 0.1% of \(A\)) or traced to named rows.

Analogy: energy accounting in an experiment: known losses (friction, heat) are measured separately, and the residual tells you whether there's an unknown effect. Where it breaks: if you "explain" a step by plugging the gap, \(\varepsilon\) is zero by construction and proves nothing.

Try it: A = $100,000, steps −$12,000, +$800, −$300; B = $88,400. Residual? (100,000 − 12,000 + 800 − 300 = 88,500; residual −$100.)

4. The investigation routine

  1. Restate the question precisely: which number, which report, which filters, which dates, which version.
  2. Reproduce both numbers yourself from their sources. Save queries and results (evidence/). This uses the read-only SQL and evidence habits from your bi-skills-kit.
  3. Bridge from one to the other, biggest suspected step first.
  4. Classify the residual: definition, timing, scope, data error.
  5. Report in writing: the bridge table, what's a definition choice (needs a business decision), what's a bug (needs a fix with a test), and the owner of each.
  6. Prevent: add a reconciliation check (control totals, source vs warehouse daily) so this class of difference is caught automatically next time.

The 5-year lens

Never say "the data is right" before the bridge is done, and never say "the data is wrong" before it's done either. Use "not yet checked" for steps you haven't evidenced. Keep a reconciliation log (date, question, outcome, cause, fix) in the repo; after six months it's a map of where your platform is fragile, and it's gold for interviews: "I kept a reconciliation log; 60% of issues were definitions, which is why I wrote the metric dictionary."

5. Certification and sign-off

For executive and finance reports: a named owner, a certified semantic model (Power BI endorsement: Certified), a daily reconciliation check to a control total (POS daily Z-report totals, PMS night-audit totals, ad platform billing), and a change process (a new definition needs the owner's sign-off, recorded in the dictionary's changelog).

  4. Write docs/reconciliation/2026-09-R104.md with the six-step routine.

Check yourself

1. Most common cause of "your number doesn't match mine"?

Definitions and timing explain most gaps; bugs are rarer.

2. A = 50,000. Steps: −6,500 tax, +400 refunds. B = 43,850. Residual?

50,000 − 6,500 + 400 = 43,900; 43,850 − 43,900 = −50.

3. Why must each bridge step be computed from data?

Evidence per step is what makes the bridge a proof.
